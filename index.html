Executive Takeaway 01

Augmentation Over Pure Automation

Aligning with Raisch & Krakowski's (2021) Automation-Augmentation Paradox, raw exposure frequency is not the psychological hazard. Well-being hinges on whether AI is deployed as a transparent co-pilot that preserves human judgment or as an opaque managerial mandate.

Executive Takeaway 02

Design for Autonomy First

Organisations investing in AI transformation must embed decision latitude, explainable outputs, and employee override rights directly into workflows. Preserving autonomy activates the motivational pathway (β=.346 → β=.717), driving sustainable engagement and mental flourishing.

CLAUDE OR FRAUD; AI'S IMPACT ON SELF-PERCEIVED CREATIVITY AND TEAMWORK

Authors: Mudatheer M, Toby N, Kaiyan M, Meghdeepsinh S

UEA
SHL

01 · Introduction

As AI reshapes SHL's People Science ecosystem, the core challenge is ensuring technology empowers rather than erodes a consultant's "creative spark." Grounded in the JD-R framework (Demerouti et al., 2001; Bakker & Demerouti, 2007), AI acts as a double-edged sword: a Job Resource when transparent, or a Hindrance Demand when creating a Trust Gap via technostress (Tarafdar et al., 2007). Anchored in Self-Determination Theory (Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness) and the AMO lens (Appelbaum et al., 2000), our model reframes AI as a collaborative partner delivering on SHL's "People science, people answer" promise.

02 · Diagnosis: Black Box vs. Glass Box AI

"Black Box" AI produces opaque outputs that limit understanding, reduce perceived control, and trigger technostress—degrading Self-Perceived Creativity (SPC). However, when SDT operates as a resource buffer (supporting autonomy, competence, and relatedness), transparent "Glass Box" AI becomes an enabler. At the team level, transparent systems strengthen task interdependence and foster collective creativity.

Critical Tension: AI vs. Creativity in People Science

03 · ADVISE: 5-Step Strategic Framework

01 EMBODY Embed an explainable "Glass Box" AI model making outputs transparent, interpretable, and open to contest.
02 INVEST Develop human-AI usability training shifting focus from passive tool use to critical interrogation and refinement.
03 RESTRUCTURE Redesign workflows around collaborative AI interpretation to reinforce task interdependence and collective creativity.
04 EVOLVE Update performance appraisal systems to reward creativity, judgement, and human-AI collaboration beyond static outputs.
05 EVALUATE Adopt continuous KPI insights and iterative feedback loops ensuring AI consistently acts as a job resource.

Human-AI Collaborative Ecosystem

AMO-driven task interdependence converting individual AI prompts into shared creative excellence.

04 · Design & Implement: 6-Week Rollout

Week / Focus Key Actions Outcome
Wk 1 · DiagnoseIdentify AI as resource vs. demand (JD-R); assess trust & friction.Clear baseline of AI impact
Wk 2 · TransparencyIntroduce Glass Box AI (reasoning, confidence); enable contestability.Trusted support tool
Wk 3 · CapabilityTrain consultants to challenge AI; embed "What's missing?" prompts.Higher competence (SDT)
Wk 4 · WorkflowLaunch collaborative AI sessions; assign generate/challenge/refine roles.Stronger interdependence
Wk 5 · PerformanceShift KPIs to idea quality + AI capability; reward critical challenge.Sustained ownership
Wk 6 · EvaluateTrack SPC, collaboration, and risk flags; iterate via R2D2 (Chen, 2008).Scalable data-driven model

05 · Multi-Level Evaluation

  • 1. Individual Level: Human-AI usability, SPC, and personal resources (Demerouti et al., 2001).
  • 2. Behavioural Level: Usage frequency, idea implementation rate, and decision quality (Aguinis & Burgi-Tian, 2021).
  • 3. Team Level: Task interdependence, collaboration quality, and cognitive diversity (Armstrong, 2017).
  • 4. Risk Monitoring: Guardrails against AI dependency, cognitive offloading, and over-reliance (Taylor et al., 1995).
  • 5. Kirkpatrick 4-Level: Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, and Results (Kirkpatrick, 1998).
  • 6. Continuous R2D2 Loop: Iterative KPI-driven refinement of training and workflows (Chen, 2008).

Conclusion

AI is not a standalone creativity engine—it is a work system facilitator. The difference between creative erosion and sustained innovation lies in work design, not the technology itself. When AI is transparent, contestable, and embedded into collaborative workflows, organisations unlock both efficiency and deeply human consulting excellence.
